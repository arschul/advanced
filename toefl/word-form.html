Word form

These errors are not about vocabulary. The student knows the word and picks the wrong ending for the slot it has to fill — which is why error-identification items lean on this family so heavily. Twelve patterns below, then thirty exercises.

The twelve patterns

The word is right. The ending is not. Ask what job the slot needs done, then build the form that does it.

1 · Adjective or adverb
The technicians recorded the readings careful.
The technicians recorded the readings carefully. · They took careful readings.

An adjective describes a noun; an adverb describes a verb, an adjective or another adverb. Find what the word is actually modifying. Careful readings modifies a noun, so no -ly; recorded carefully modifies a verb, so -ly is required.

2 · Adjective after linking verbs
The results seem conclusively.
The results seem conclusive. · The evidence looks strong and the argument sounds reasonable.

After be, seem, appear, become, remain, look, sound, feel, taste, smell, prove the word describes the subject, not the action, so it stays an adjective. Test by substituting is: if the results are conclusive works, the adjective is right.

3 · Noun where an adjective is needed
measures intended to stimulate economy growth
measures intended to stimulate economic growth · the economy of the region

A word sitting directly in front of a noun usually has to be an adjective. The trap is that some nouns can modify other nouns (government policy, research funding), so students assume all of them can. When a matching adjective exists — economic, political, industrial, cultural — that adjective is the one the slot wants.

4 · Noun or verb form
The analyze of the data took three months.
The analysis of the data took three months. · They will analyze the data in March.

After the, a, this, its or any other determiner, only a noun can follow. Pairs worth drilling because the noun is irregular: analyze / analysis, decide / decision, describe / description, succeed / success, prove / proof, choose / choice, lose / loss, believe / belief.

5 · -ed and -ing adjectives
The students found the conclusion confused.
The students found the conclusion confusing. · The students were confused by the conclusion.

-ing describes the thing that causes the feeling; -ed describes the person who has it. A lecture is boring, an audience is bored. The full set: interesting/interested, confusing/confused, surprising/surprised, tiring/tired, frustrating/frustrated, worrying/worried.

6 · Noun suffixes
praised the efficient with which the staff handled the merger
praised the efficiency with which the staff handled the merger

The endings that build nouns: -tion (protection), -ment (agreement), -ance / -ence (resistance, independence), -ity (complexity), -ness (awareness), -ship (leadership), -cy (efficiency), -al (approval), -ure (failure).

7 · Adjective suffixes
The proposal was more persuasion than anyone expected.
The proposal was more persuasive than anyone expected.

The endings that build adjectives: -al (national), -ive (persuasive), -ous (dangerous), -ful (successful), -less (harmless), -able / -ible (reliable, responsible), -ic (economic), -ent / -ant (dependent, significant). After more, very, quite, too and after a linking verb, an adjective is what belongs.

8 · Verb-forming suffixes
regulations intended to strength the protections workers won
regulations intended to strengthen the protections workers won

The endings that build verbs: -en (strengthen, widen, shorten), -ize (modernize, prioritize), -ify (simplify, clarify), -ate (differentiate). After to, after a modal, or in any main-verb slot, the noun form cannot stand in.

9 · Confusable pairs
The delay did not effect the outcome.
The delay did not affect the outcome. · The delay had no effect on the outcome.

Affect is nearly always the verb, effect nearly always the noun. Others that turn up: advice (noun) / advise (verb), rise (no object) / raise (takes one), economic (of the economy) / economical (thrifty), historic (momentous) / historical (of the past), sensible (reasonable) / sensitive (easily affected).

10 · Adverbs that break the -ly rule
The committee had hard begun its review when the funding was withdrawn.
The committee had hardly begun its review. · The committee worked hard on the review.

Some pairs look related and are not. Hard means with effort, hardly means almost not. Late means not on time, lately means recently. Near is close by, nearly is almost. Note also that good is only an adjective — the adverb is well — and that friendly, likely, lively end in -ly but are adjectives.

11 · The slot decides the form
To success in graduate study requires more than talent.
Success in graduate study requires more than talent. · Succeeding in graduate study requires more than talent.

A subject must be a noun or a gerund. Work through the slots in order: after a determiner, a noun; after be or seem, an adjective; modifying a verb, an adverb; in the subject or object slot, a noun. Identify the slot before you pick the ending.

12 · Noun modifiers stay singular
The ministry approved a five-years plan.
The ministry approved a five-year plan. · The plan runs for five years.

A noun used to modify another noun loses its plural: a two-hour meeting, a ten-page report, a three-day conference. The plural returns as soon as the noun stands on its own again. The hyphen is a reliable signal that the singular is required.
