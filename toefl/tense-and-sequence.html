Verb tense and sequence

Choosing a tense is rarely the problem. Holding two tenses in the right relationship is — which event came first, what a time marker commits you to, what happens to a tense when it is reported. Twelve patterns below, then thirty exercises: choose the form, find the error, correct and classify.

The twelve patterns

Fix the timeline first, then choose the forms that express it.

1 · Present perfect or simple past
The treatment has been discovered in 2018.
The treatment was discovered in 2018. · The treatment has been in use for eight years.

A finished time expression — in 2018, yesterday, last month, two years ago, during the war — forces the simple past. The present perfect needs time that is unfinished or unstated: recently, so far, already, yet, ever, never, since, this year.

2 · Since and for
He has worked here since three years.
He has worked here for three years. · He has worked here since 2023.

For takes a length of time, since takes the point it started. Since also introduces a clause in the simple past while the main verb stays perfect: Since she joined the department, output has doubled.

3 · Past perfect for the earlier of two past events
By the time the auditors arrived, the manager destroyed the files.
By the time the auditors arrived, the manager had destroyed the files.

Two past events, one clearly first: the earlier one takes had + past participle. Triggered by by the time, before, after, already, until then, previously. Where the order is obvious from the sequence alone, the simple past is enough: She opened the door and walked out.

4 · Backshift in reported speech
She said that she will send the manuscript.
She said that she would send the manuscript. · He told us he had finished.

After a past reporting verb, each tense moves one step back: present → past, past → past perfect, will → would, can → could, must → had to. Time words shift too: tomorrow → the following day, now → then, yesterday → the day before. A permanent truth may stay unshifted: He said that water boils at 100°C.

5 · Present forms after time conjunctions
We will inform you as soon as we will receive confirmation.
We will inform you as soon as we receive confirmation.

After when, as soon as, before, after, until, once, by the time, future meaning is carried by a present form. Use the present perfect for a completed step: Once you have submitted the form, you will receive a code. The exception is when meaning at what time in a direct or indirect question, which does take will.

6 · Conditional sequences
If she would have studied the manual, she would not have damaged the equipment.
If she had studied the manual, she would not have damaged the equipment.

The three pairs TOEFL tests: if + present → will; if + past → would; if + had done → would have done. Would never appears in the if-clause. Watch for mixed conditionals, where a past condition has a present result: If they had invested earlier, the company would be profitable now.

7 · Wish, if only, would rather, it's time
I wish I would have accepted the offer last spring.
I wish I had accepted the offer last spring. · I wish I had more time now.

Regret about the past takes the past perfect; a wish about the present takes the simple past. Wish + would is reserved for complaints about other people's behavior: I wish he would stop interrupting. The same past-for-present logic drives if only, would rather you came and it's time we left.

8 · Perfect continuous for duration
The department is offering the course since 2018.
The department has been offering the course since 2018.

Use the perfect continuous when an activity ran up to now and the duration matters. Use the plain perfect when the result matters or the amount is counted: She has written three chapters (result) against She has been writing all morning (duration).

9 · Stative verbs take simple forms
She is knowing the material well. · The university is owning several buildings.
She knows the material well. · The university owns several buildings.

Verbs of thinking, feeling, possession and perception avoid the continuous: know, believe, understand, own, belong, contain, consist, seem, appear, prefer, need, resemble. A few change meaning rather than break the rule: I am thinking about it (activity) against I think it is wrong (opinion).

10 · Future perfect and future continuous
By 2035 the region will lose half of its glaciers.
By 2035 the region will have lost half of its glaciers.

By + a future point calls for the future perfect: the action is complete before that moment. The future continuous marks something in progress then: This time next week we will be presenting the results. Keep both apart from going to for present evidence and intention, and will for a decision made as you speak.

11 · Used to, would, be used to
Workers were used to receive their wages in cash.
Workers used to receive their wages in cash. · Workers are used to receiving their wages in cash.

Three separate structures. Used to + base form is a discontinued past habit or state. Be used to + -ing means accustomed to and works in any tense. Would + base form also narrates repeated past actions, but not past states: She would walk to school is fine, she would live there is not.

12 · Consistency within a passage
The narrator describes his childhood, recalls the summers by the sea, and then shifted to his adult life.
The narrator describes his childhood, recalls the summers by the sea, and then shifts to his adult life.

Do not change tense mid-passage unless the time frame really changes. Two conventions matter for TOEFL writing: plots, arguments and what a text says are described in the present (the study argues, the novel opens), while what researchers did and found is reported in the past (Kuhn surveyed 400 cases).
