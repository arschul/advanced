Verb forms and non-finite clauses

Tense tells you when something happened. This sheet is about how the verb is put together: the auxiliary chains behind modals, perfects and passives, and the participles left behind when a clause loses its subject. Twelve patterns below, then thirty exercises.

The twelve patterns

Every auxiliary fixes the form of the word after it. Build the chain from the left and the last word comes out right.

1 · Modal + base form
The ministry ruled that all schools must to adopt the new curriculum.
All schools must adopt the new curriculum. · The schools should have adopted it sooner.

After can, could, may, might, will, would, shall, should, must and had better the verb takes its bare form — no to, no -s, no -ed, no -ing. The modal carries the tense, so a past meaning is built with have plus the past participle: must have adopted, should have known. Note that ought to, have to and be able to keep their to, which is what makes the others feel unstable.

2 · Perfect: have + past participle
The engineers had went over the specifications twice.
The engineers had gone over the specifications twice. · The report has been written.

Have, has and had are followed by the past participle, never by the simple past. The irregular verbs where the two forms differ are where the error lives: go / went / gone, write / wrote / written, take / took / taken, choose / chose / chosen, begin / began / begun, rise / rose / risen, break / broke / broken, drink / drank / drunk. If the verb has three distinct forms, the third one is the one that follows have.

3 · Passive: be + past participle
The tomb was discover in 1908.
The tomb was discovered in 1908. · The survey is conducted every spring.

A passive is a form of be plus the past participle, and both halves have to be there. Two errors are common: leaving the verb in its base form (was discover) and using the -ing form (was discovering), which is active and means something quite different. Check the subject — if it is the thing done to rather than the doer, the sentence is passive and the participle is required.

4 · Passive with modals and perfect forms
The results should have been publish in the spring.
The results should have been published in the spring. · The samples must be kept below four degrees.

However long the chain of auxiliaries, each one fixes the form of the next, and the last word is always a past participle: must be kept, has been sent, will have been completed, could be published. Build the chain from the left: the modal takes a bare form (be, have), have takes been, be takes the participle.

5 · Questions and negatives take do + base form
The minister did not announced the closure.
The minister did not announce the closure. · Did the committee meet in June?

Do, does and did carry the tense, so the main verb returns to its base form. The same rule governs emphasis (she does know) and question tags. Where an auxiliary is already present, do is not used at all: has not arrived, not does not has arrived.

6 · Verbs that have no passive
The earthquake was happened at dawn.
The earthquake happened at dawn. · The population rose sharply after 1950.

A verb that takes no object cannot be turned around, so it has no passive. The ones that appear in test items: happen, occur, take place, arrive, appear, disappear, exist, consist of, belong to, rise, fall, seem, remain, die. Watch the pairs where one member takes an object and the other does not, since only the first has a passive: rise / raise, lie / lay, fall / fell.

7 · Make, let, help and have
The director made the interns to record every call.
The director made the interns record every call. · She had the report rewritten by an editor.

Make, let and have take a bare infinitive after the object; help takes either (helped them finish or helped them to finish). Have and get also build a causative passive when the object is a thing rather than a person: have the report rewritten, get the roof repaired. Be made, though, restores the to: the interns were made to record every call.

8 · Gerund or infinitive after the verb
The board considered to postpone the vote.
The board considered postponing the vote. · The board decided to postpone the vote.

The verb in front decides. Gerund: avoid, consider, deny, delay, postpone, suggest, risk, mind, finish, enjoy, admit, recommend. Infinitive: decide, agree, refuse, offer, promise, manage, fail, hope, intend, expect. After any preposition the gerund is the only option, which is why the to in look forward to, object to and be accustomed to catches people out: look forward to receiving, not to receive.

9 · Reduction with -ing
Having been reviewed the applications for six weeks, the committee announced a shortlist.
Having reviewed the applications for six weeks, the committee announced a shortlist. · Realizing the error, the auditor withdrew the claim.

When the subject of a clause matches the subject of the main clause, the clause can shed its subject and its tense: after she had reviewed becomes having reviewed. Use the -ing form where that subject performs the action. Having plus a past participle marks the earlier of two events; the plain -ing form marks actions that overlap. The subject still has to match — that trap is Sheet 8, pattern 1.

10 · Reduction with -ed
Writing in Latin and circulated only in manuscript, the treatise reached few readers.
Written in Latin and circulated only in manuscript, the treatise reached few readers. · Built of limestone, the bridge survived the flood.

Where the subject receives the action rather than performs it, the reduction leaves a past participle: because it was written becomes written. Ask whether the subject did it or had it done. Signed in 1923, the treaty still holds. A being can be added where the point is an event rather than a state: being questioned by the police, he said nothing.

11 · Reduced relative clauses
The students who sitting at the back could not hear.
The students sitting at the back could not hear. · The students who were sitting at the back could not hear.

A relative clause containing a form of be can drop the pronoun and the be together — but not one without the other: the letters that were discovered becomes the letters discovered; the delegates who are meeting becomes the delegates meeting. Where there is no be, an -ing form still works if the meaning is active: people who live here becomes people living here. What never works is a pronoun left in front of a bare participle.

12 · Reduced adverb clauses keep their conjunction
Before submitted the proposal, the department consulted two committees.
Before submitting the proposal, the department consulted two committees. · Although warned twice, the crew continued.

When an adverb clause is reduced, the conjunction stays and only the subject and the auxiliary go: while she was searching becomes while searching; although they were warned becomes although warned. Choose the participle by voice, exactly as in patterns 9 and 10. The conjunctions that allow this: while, when, before, after, since, although, though, if, unless, once. Because does not — it gives way to because of plus a noun, or the clause drops the conjunction altogether.
