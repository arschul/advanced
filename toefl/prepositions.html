Prepositions and dependent prepositions

Almost nothing here can be reasoned out. A verb, an adjective or a noun simply owns its preposition, and the pair has to be learned whole — which is why this family rewards drilling more than any other on the list. Twelve patterns below, then thirty exercises.

The twelve patterns

Most of these are not rules. They are pairs, and pairs have to be learned as pairs.

1 · Verb + preposition pairs
The outcome depends of the resources the department allocates.
The outcome depends on the resources the department allocates.

The pairs TOEFL returns to: depend on, consist of, result in, result from, account for, focus on, rely on, refer to, contribute to, apply for, apply to, insist on, object to, succeed in, participate in, comply with, deal with, cope with, benefit from. Learn the verb and its preposition as one unit, because the preposition rarely translates.

2 · Verbs that take no preposition at all
The panel discussed about the proposal and entered into the hall.
The panel discussed the proposal and entered the hall.

These take a direct object with nothing between: discuss, enter, approach, answer, attend, emphasize, mention, lack, resemble, marry, reach, request, join, contact. The extra preposition comes straight from Portuguese and is the most common single error in this family. Note the contrast with enter into an agreement, which is a fixed phrase with a different meaning.

3 · Adjective + preposition
She is responsible of the reports and capable to solve the problem.
She is responsible for the reports and capable of solving the problem.

Fixed pairs again: interested in, capable of, responsible for, similar to, different from, aware of, concerned about, dependent on, familiar with, satisfied with, opposed to, accustomed to, superior to. Whatever follows the preposition must be a noun or a gerund, never an infinitive.

4 · Noun + preposition
The article reports a sharp decline of enrollment.
The article reports a sharp decline in enrollment. · a solution to the problem

Movement in a quantity takes in: an increase in, a decline in, a rise in, a reduction in, a change in. Others to memorize: a solution to, a reason for, an effect on, an influence on, research on, an answer to, a demand for, an approach to.

5 · In, on and at for time
The conference will take place at March.
The conference will take place in March, on a Monday, at nine in the morning.

Widest to narrowest. In for years, months, seasons, centuries and parts of the day: in 2019, in March, in the morning. On for days and dates: on Monday, on March 3. At for clock times and two fixed exceptions: at night, at the weekend. No preposition at all before last, next, this, every: last March, not in last March.

6 · In, on and at for place
The office is located at the fourth floor.
The office is located on the fourth floor of a building in the city center.

In for enclosed spaces and areas: in the building, in Curitiba, in a car. On for surfaces, floors, streets and transport lines: on the third floor, on the wall, on a bus. At for points and addresses: at the corner, at 40 Rua XV, at the station.

7 · For, since, during, by and until
He has worked here during three years and must reply until Friday.
He has worked here for three years and must reply by Friday.

For takes a length of time, since takes a starting point, during takes a named period rather than a number: during the war, not during three years. By means at or before a deadline; until means the state continues up to that point. Submit it by Friday and wait until Friday say different things.

8 · Between and among
The prize was shared between the six researchers who contributed.
The prize was shared among the six researchers. · The difference between the two methods is small.

Between for two, or for items considered individually and named. Among for an undifferentiated group of three or more. Keep the pairs intact too: between X and Y, never between X to Y; and from 1990 to 2005, never from 1990 until 2005 in a date range.

9 · A preposition is followed by a gerund
The students look forward to receive the results.
The students look forward to receiving the results. · before submitting, instead of waiting, capable of solving

Anything after a preposition is a noun or a gerund. The trap is the expressions where to is a preposition and not an infinitive marker: look forward to, be used to, get used to, object to, be accustomed to, in addition to, be committed to. Test it by substituting a noun \u2014 look forward to the results works, so the gerund is right.

10 · Different from, compared with, according to
The findings are quite different than those the earlier study reported.
The findings are quite different from those the earlier study reported.

Different from is the form TOEFL expects, though different than is common in spoken American English. Others in the comparison family: compared with or compared to, in contrast to, similar to, according to (never according with), the same as (never the same than).

11 · Phrasal verbs and their particles
The team carried on the experiment the ministry approved.
The team carried out the experiment. · They carried on working despite the delay.

The particle carries the meaning, so swapping it changes the verb entirely: carry out is to perform, carry on is to continue. Others worth drilling: point out, bring up, put off, set up, take on, look into, come across, turn down. Separable ones split around a pronoun: put it off, never put off it.

12 · Fixed phrases
In the contrary, the second experiment produced the opposite result.
On the contrary, the second experiment produced the opposite result.

These have no logic to derive; they are memorized whole: in my opinion, in general, in fact, in addition, in particular, in contrast, on the whole, on the contrary, on the other hand, on purpose, on behalf of, at least, at first, at present, by contrast, by chance, for example. Note that on the contrary corrects a statement while on the other hand introduces a different side \u2014 they are not interchangeable.
