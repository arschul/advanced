Articles, determiners and countability

English asks a question before every noun that many languages never ask: is this one of many, or a substance? Get that wrong and the article, the plural and the quantifier all go wrong together. Twelve patterns below, then thirty exercises.

The twelve patterns

Decide what kind of noun you have before you decide which word goes in front of it.

1 · Uncountable nouns take no plural and no a
The professor gave us several advices. · They ordered a new equipment.
The professor gave us a great deal of advice. · They ordered new equipment.

The list to drill, because English treats these as substances rather than objects: advice, information, equipment, research, evidence, furniture, homework, luggage, machinery, money, news, progress, traffic, vocabulary, work, knowledge, feedback, training. None takes -s and none takes a. Portuguese counts several of them, which is why they surface constantly in Brazilian students' writing.

2 · Counting an uncountable noun
She gave me three advices about the application.
She gave me three pieces of advice about the application.

Use a partitive when you need a number: a piece of advice, an item of furniture, a bit of research, a body of evidence, a great deal of information, two loaves of bread. Vaguer quantities work directly: much advice, a lot of information, some evidence, little progress.

3 · A or an goes by sound, not by letter
She completed a MBA and waited a hour for the results.
She completed an MBA and waited an hour for the results. · a university, a European partner, a one-way street

Say the next word aloud. MBA starts with the sound "em", hour with a vowel, so both take an. University starts with a "yoo" sound and one with a "w" sound, so both take a despite their spelling.

4 · First mention and later mention
A student submitted a proposal, and a proposal has already been reviewed.
A student submitted a proposal, and the proposal has already been reviewed.

Introduce with a or an, refer back with the. Once the reader knows which one you mean — because you said so, or because the situation makes it obvious — the article becomes the and stays there.

5 · The with unique reference
This is a third time the committee has rejected the application.
This is the third time the committee has rejected the application.

Anything that can only be one thing takes the: superlatives (the best), ordinals (the first, the third), and the same, the only, the next, the last, the main, the entire. Also the singular unique nouns: the sun, the government, the internet, the environment.

6 · Zero article for general statements
The good roads are essential to any exporting economy.
Good roads are essential to any exporting economy. · Governments should invest in education.

When you mean a category in general, use a plural or an uncountable noun with no article at all. Adding the narrows it to a specific set the reader is supposed to recognize — which is why the students need support and students need support say different things. This is the single most common article error in TOEFL essays.

7 · The when a modifier specifies which
Price of oil rose after the ministry announced the tariffs.
The price of oil rose after the ministry announced the tariffs. · The students who arrived late missed the briefing.

The mirror image of pattern 6. An of-phrase or a defining relative clause pins the noun down to a particular one, and a pinned-down noun takes the. Compare general oil prices rose with specific the price of oil rose.

8 · Much, many, fewer, less, amount, number
Less students applied, and the amount of applications fell.
Fewer students applied, and the number of applications fell.

Countable plurals take many, fewer, a number of; uncountables take much, less, an amount of. Test it by asking whether you could count the noun one by one: fewer students but less funding, the number of errors but the amount of evidence.

9 · A few and few, a little and little
She has a few friends in the city, so she rarely goes out.
She has few friends in the city, so she rarely goes out. · She has a few friends there, so she goes out often.

Dropping the article reverses the meaning. A few and a little are positive — some, enough. Few and little are negative — hardly any, not enough. The rest of the sentence usually tells you which the writer meant, and that is what TOEFL items test.

10 · Place names and institutions
He returned from the Peru and went straight to the university to register.
He returned from Peru and went straight to the university nearest his home.

No the with most countries, cities, states, lakes, single mountains or continents: Brazil, Curitiba, Lake Titicaca, Mount Everest. Use the with plurals and political unions: the United States, the Netherlands, the Philippines, and with rivers, seas, deserts and ranges: the Amazon, the Andes, the Sahara. Institutions used for their purpose take no article — at school, in class, at work — though American English keeps it in in the hospital.

11 · Determiners fix the noun's number
Every students must complete the modules. · Both proposal were rejected.
Every student must complete the modules. · Both proposals were rejected.

Singular noun after each, every, either, neither, another. Plural noun after both, several, many, few, all and after one of. All and most also take uncountables directly: most research, all evidence.

12 · Other, another, the other
One candidate withdrew and the another declined the offer.
One candidate withdrew and the other declined the offer. · One withdrew and another may follow.

Another already contains an, so it never follows the. Use another for one more of many, the other for the remaining one of two, the others for all the rest, and bare other before a plural: other studies disagree. Two determiners never stack: not the my report, not a some evidence.
